# pharmacovigilance-task1
# Task 1: ADR Identification Basics Case Evaluation

## 1. Patient Information & Demographics
* **Patient:** Female, 45 years old
* **Presenting Symptoms:** Skin rash, itching (pruritus), nausea, and dizziness
* **Medication History:**
  * **Amoxicillin:** Initiated 5 days ago
  * **Paracetamol:** Taken as needed

---

## 2. Timeline & Onset Analysis
* **Day -5:** Initiated oral therapy with Amoxicillin.
* **Day -2:** Onset of symptoms (rash, itching, nausea, dizziness) 3 days after starting Amoxicillin.
* **Day 0:** Presentation for evaluation.

---

## 3. Causality & Suspected Drug Identification
* **Primary Suspected Drug:** **Amoxicillin**
* **Likelihood / Reaction Type:** 
  * **Type I / Type IV Hypersensitivity Reaction:** Cutaneous reactions (maculopapular rash, pruritus) are well-documented allergic responses to beta-lactam antibiotics like Amoxicillin.
  * **Temporal Sequence:** Positive — Symptoms emerged 3 days into antibiotic administration.
* **Concomitant Drug (Paracetamol):** Taken as needed; significantly less likely to cause a delayed widespread rash compared to an active systemic antibiotic course.

---

## 4. Action Plan & Recommendations
1. **Discontinuation:** Stop Amoxicillin immediately under physician guidance to prevent progression to severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCARs).
2. **Alternative Therapy:** Consult the prescribing provider to switch to an alternative non-beta-lactam antibiotic class (e.g., macrolide).
3. **Symptom Management:** Administer oral antihistamines for pruritus and supportive therapy for nausea.
4. **Documentation:** Update the patient's Electronic Health Record (EHR) to flag a penicillin allergy to prevent future re-exposure.
